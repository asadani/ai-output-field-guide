Contracts & knowledge · Provenance model

W3C PROV

Describe the entities, activities, and agents involved in producing an artifact.

Intermediate · 3 drills · Reviewed 2026-10-24

See an examplePractise

What it helps you do

Let someone inspect how a result relates to inputs, transformations, and responsible actors.

Starting knowledge: No specialist knowledge is required.

  • Identify the artifact and its source entities.
  • Record the activity that used inputs and generated an output.
  • Identify responsible agents and preserve version or revision references.

The problem it came from

The W3C PROV family was published in 2013 to support interoperable provenance information. It includes a conceptual model, ontology, and serializations.

The overview distinguishes information about a thing’s production from an automatic verdict about its trustworthiness.

Where it helps. Where it stops.

Use it when

  • A report combines data from several sources or transformations.
  • An AI-generated result needs a traceable connection to supplied evidence.

Potential benefits

  • Provenance helps a reviewer reconstruct dependencies and origin.
  • Versioned inputs can reveal when an artifact may need revisiting.

Limits and common mistakes

  • A provenance record can be incomplete or false.
  • Knowing where a claim came from does not prove the source or transformation was correct.

DRILL 03 Not attempted

An input changes after a report was generated. What should the provenance let a reader discover?

Hint

What would a reviewer need before reusing the report?

Explained answer & self-review rubric

Which revision the report actually used, which parts depend on that input, and whether any review against the new revision has occurred. A source change is a reason to inspect freshness, not proof that every statement became false.

  • Retain the old input revision
  • Expose dependency
  • Avoid equating change with certain invalidity
