Plain language

Help the intended reader find, understand, and use the information they need.

Introductory · 3 drills · Reviewed 2026-10-24

See an examplePractise

What it helps you do

Make a document serve its reader’s real situation, including navigation, relevance, vocabulary, and action.

Starting knowledge: No specialist knowledge is required.

  • Identify the reader and the question they need answered.
  • Put necessary information where the reader expects to find it.
  • Use familiar wording while explaining indispensable technical terms.

The problem it came from

Plain-language practice spans public communication, administration, and technical writing. ISO 24495-1:2023 established international governing principles and guidelines.

The standard addresses primarily textual information and applies across written languages. This page uses its public description and original examples; it does not reproduce the standard.

Work through an example

STARTING POINT
Pursuant to the revised attendance provisions, participants are requested to effect registration prior to the indicated closing date.
APPLYING THE APPROACH
Register for the workshop by Friday, 5 June. Use the registration form. If you need help completing it, contact the organizer.
  • The action and deadline come first.
  • The support route is stated instead of assuming every reader can use the form.
  • The date and contact route are example facts and must be supplied for real use.

Where it helps. Where it stops.

Use it when

  • Public notices, event instructions, guidance pages, and explanations for a new audience.
  • A document is accurate but readers cannot find the action or deadline.

Potential benefits

  • Task-centered ordering can make a document easier to use without removing detail.
  • Explicit definitions support access for readers outside the author’s specialty.

Limits and common mistakes

  • Plain language does not mean writing for children or imposing one reading age.
  • A readability score does not establish usefulness, accessibility, or factual accuracy.

DRILL 01 Not attempted

Which question should come first when rewriting a notice?

Choose one answer

Hint

Start with the reader’s situation.

Explained answer

Best answer for this case: Who needs this information and what will they do with it?

Purpose and audience guide selection, order, and vocabulary. Sentence length is only one consideration.

Explained answer

Best answer for this case: Information needed to act

DRILL 03 Not attempted

A newcomer cannot find where to cancel a booking. What would you change before rewriting every sentence?

Hint

Consider structure before word substitution.

Explained answer & self-review rubric

Add a clearly labeled cancellation route where booking information appears; state the deadline, consequences, and help route. Then test whether a newcomer can complete the task.

  • Improve findability
  • Preserve conditions and consequences
  • Check actual use with a reader
