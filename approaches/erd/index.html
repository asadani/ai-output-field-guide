Entity–relationship modeling

Describe entities, relationships, and cardinality before designing or interpreting data.

Intermediate · 3 drills · Reviewed 2026-10-24

See an examplePractise

What it helps you do

Make it clear which things are distinct and how many of one may relate to another.

Starting knowledge: Some familiarity with records and identifiers helps.

  • Identify entities and their identities.
  • Name relationships and specify minimum and maximum participation.
  • Separate a conceptual relationship from its storage implementation.

The problem it came from

Peter Chen’s 1976 paper introduced the entity–relationship model as a unified view of data. Later notations, including crow’s foot, express similar relationship questions with different symbols.

An ER model describes the domain or data structure. A database implementation adds decisions about keys, constraints, storage, and transactions.

Work through an example

STARTING POINT
People book workshops. Store the person’s name next to the workshop name.
APPLYING THE APPROACH
Participant (participant_id)
Workshop (workshop_id)
Registration (registration_id, participant_id, workshop_id)

A participant can have zero or many registrations.
A workshop can have zero or many registrations.
Each registration refers to exactly one participant and one workshop.
  • Registration represents the association and can carry its own state.
  • Names are attributes, not necessarily stable identifiers.
  • Preventing duplicate active registrations requires an additional rule.

Where it helps. Where it stops.

Use it when

  • Designing a registration system or understanding an existing data model.
  • Ambiguous terms hide whether two records are different entities or repeated descriptions.

Potential benefits

  • Cardinality exposes assumptions that ordinary nouns leave implicit.
  • An explicit relationship model helps inspect duplicate and missing-record cases.

Limits and common mistakes

  • A diagram does not enforce database constraints.
  • Data relationships alone do not define authorization, temporal rules, or process order.

DRILL 01 Not attempted

Why introduce Registration between Participant and Workshop?

Choose one answer

Hint

Can the relationship itself have properties?

Explained answer

Best answer for this case: To represent a relationship that can have its own identity and attributes

A registration may carry status and timestamps. An association entity can model those facts without pretending every relationship needs one.

DRILL 02 Not attempted

Does an ER diagram alone prevent two active registrations for the same person and workshop?

Choose one answer

Hint

Description and enforcement differ.

Explained answer

Best answer for this case: No; a uniqueness rule and enforcement are also needed

The model can state the rule, but the database and application must enforce it.

DRILL 03 Not attempted

Explain why a person’s name is a poor default primary identity.

Hint

Can a label be non-unique or mutable?

Explained answer & self-review rubric

Different people can share a name and one person’s name can change. Use a stable identifier and treat the name as an attribute, subject to the domain’s actual identity rules.

  • Consider collisions
  • Consider changes
  • Separate identity from presentation
